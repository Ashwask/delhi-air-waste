Delhi Air + Waste: A Systemic Frame

Interlock as the spine. Leverage as the logic. Partners as the operators. First stab for RF + RM · Draft v0.2 · 13 Sep 2026 · built to iterate

Base premise (from Delhi air pollution research.docx): "At first glance, air pollution and municipal solid waste seem like two distinct environmental problems. In Delhi they are interlocking crises... bound by the same structural constraints: unfavorable geography, transboundary jurisdictional friction, and an administrative focus on temporary emergency measures over long-term systemic change." This frame takes that thesis and makes it operational: it shows the system, finds the leverage, and names who acts.

1. The reframe (the one idea)

Delhi manages air and waste as two separate, downstream, seasonal emergencies. The data says they are one system with a shared upstream cause: unsegregated, unmanaged material and uncombusted-cleanly fuel, whose emissions are then concentrated by winter physics into the crisis we see.

The systemic error we are correcting: effort and money sit downstream and seasonal (GRAP, smog towers, biomining, dust-spraying) while the leverage sits upstream and year-round (segregation, source control, clean combustion, and the money that drives them). Move effort upstream and one fix breaks many loops.

2. The interlock, as a system

The same failure points (no segregation, dumping, open burning, fires, dirty WtE) feed the same trap (winter boundary-layer collapse + regional airshed) that produces the same harm. The current response acts on the trap and the symptoms, never on the sources, so the loop repeats every year. The four moves (Section 4) mark where we break it.

Read left to right: SOURCES to FAILURE POINTS to THE SHARED TRAP to HARM, with today's downstream RESPONSE feeding a loop that repeats every winter. The leverage bar at top shows why upstream action beats downstream reaction.

3. Where the leverage is (effort vs impact are mismatched)

The higher upstream you act, the more of the system you move. Today's spending is inverted.

Layer What lives here Systemic leverage Where the effort/money actually goes today
Upstream (sources + money) Segregation, source separation, clean combustion, the incentives behind them Highest (breaks many streams at once) Neglected: segregation stuck ~55%; contractors still paid to haul, not to divert
Midstream (capacity) Processing, MRFs, formal recyclers, C&D recycling Medium Partial: ~64% processed; 4,200 TPD gap; informal sector excluded
Downstream (symptoms) GRAP, smog towers, dust-spraying, biomining Lowest (treats effects) Most of it: 64 to 67% of NCAP spend on road dust; ₹19.42 cr smog tower now non-functional

The task-force thesis in one line: shift Delhi's effort up the leverage ladder, from reacting to the trap toward closing the sources and fixing the money that feeds them, year-round instead of every November.

4. The four systemic moves (the backbone)

Each move breaks multiple loops at once. Everything in the action/partner map (Section 5) rolls up into one of these.

MOVE A: Close the front end (segregation + source separation)

  • Why systemic: one lever improves wet (kills methane + fires), dry/plastic/e-waste (kills open burning + recovers value), lowers landfill inflow, and raises WtE calorific value. The single highest-leverage point in the system.
  • Data backing: segregation ~55%; wet is ~50% of ~11,850 TPD; open burning + landfill methane are direct PM/GHG sources.
  • Partners: MCD + RWAs + bulk generators; Daily Dump, Saahas Zero Waste, GPS Renewables (bio-CNG); Chintan.

MOVE B: Formalise the informal system (cheap latent capacity)

  • Why systemic: the informal sector already diverts 15 to 20% of recyclables for free across dry, plastic and e-waste. Formalising it adds capacity across three streams at near-zero capital cost and creates jobs.
  • Data backing: 150,000+ waste pickers; Chintan/Safai Sena recycle >2,000 TPD, saving ~₹1 cr/day for civic bodies.
  • Partners: Chintan / Safai Sena; MCD; EPR players (Recykal, Karo Sambhav); Toxics Link (e-waste).

MOVE C: Make the system visible (measurement + accountability)

  • Why systemic: you cannot shift from reactive to source-based action if the data is untrusted or missing. This move unlocks the other three by making sources measurable and owners accountable.
  • Data backing: CAG 2025 found stations mis-sited and AQI unreliable; no biomedical data post-2017; plastic tonnage contested 690 vs 1,145 TPD; composition dated to 2005.
  • Partners: CPCB, CSTEP, CEEW; sensor firms (Respirer, Aurassure); a fresh waste-characterisation study.

MOVE D: Fix the money (redirect incentives upstream)

  • Why systemic: the root cause under every stream is that the money rewards the wrong behaviour. Contractors are paid per tonne hauled (rewarding dumping over recovery), landfilling costs ~₹14,500/tonne, and two-thirds of clean-air spend goes downstream to dust. Redirect the money and A, B and C become self-sustaining instead of grant-dependent.
  • Data backing: perverse per-tonne-hauled tipping fees (base doc); NCAP ₹7,594 cr of ₹11,211 cr utilised, 64 to 67% to road dust; NGT fines (₹900 cr, ₹2,232 cr) sit unlinked to remediation; EPR funds under-deployed.
  • The levers: end per-tonne-hauled contracts (pay for diversion/processing outcomes); channel EPR fees into stream fixes; use NGT fines as remediation capital; viability-gap funding for composting and recyclers; performance-linked municipal transfers.
  • Partners: MCD/DPCC (contracting), CAQM/MoEFCC (NCAP + EPR), 15th Finance Commission flows, producers (EPR), philanthropic/blended capital (where RF + RM can catalyse).

6. The givens (system boundary: design around, do not chase)

  • Winter meteorology / boundary-layer collapse: physics; reduce the emissions that get trapped, not the trap.
  • Regional airshed (~65 to 70% of winter PM2.5): needs CAQM inter-state action, not a Delhi pilot.
  • Legacy stock drawdown: multi-year, already underway.

(These are the same three structural constraints the base doc names: geography, jurisdiction, and reactive-emergency governance. We treat the first two as givens and make the third, reactive governance, the thing we change.)

7. How to read this as a task force

  1. The spine is the interlock (Section 2): agree it is one system.
  2. The strategy is to move up the leverage ladder (Section 3).
  3. The backbone is four moves (Section 4): pick where to start.
  4. The operating sheet is the map (Section 5): assign partners.
  5. The discipline is honesty about the givens (Section 6) and the data gaps (fresh characterisation, biomedical data, plastic tonnage, monitoring integrity).

Open iteration questions for us: Is "shift effort upstream" the right organising thesis? Are A/B/C/D the right four (D = financing was just added)? And which of these does the government actually want to own?
