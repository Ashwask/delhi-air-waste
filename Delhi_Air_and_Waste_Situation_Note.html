Delhi Air + Waste: Situation Note

A shared problem baseline for the proposed RF + RM task force

Draft v1 · 13 September 2026

Assumptions flagged for correction: "RF" = Rainmatter Foundation, "RM" = Rainmatter. This note is written to serve two readers at once: the government counterparts who asked for help, and the task force of partners we are assembling. It deliberately separates what is known from what is contested, and what is in our control from what is not. Full data and citations sit in the companion file Delhi_Air_and_Waste_Sourced_Brief.md.

1. Purpose and framing

Government counterparts have signalled interest in support on Delhi's waste + air quality challenge. Before scoping any initiative, we need a common, honest picture of the problem. This note is that foundation.

Its job is to: 1. Define the problem in air and waste, with data, plainly enough for a mixed room. 2. Separate the givens from the controllables, so a task force does not try to boil the ocean. 3. Decompose the waste, stream by stream, because "waste" is not one problem but ten, each with a different owner, flow, and failure point. 4. Map who already does what, so we can see where our connections add leverage. 5. Set a structure for turning this into implementable, buy-in-friendly solutions with partners.

Worst case, this is an exercise to understand the true extent of the issue and surface solutions that can win buy-in. Best case, it becomes an RF + RM-convened effort that helps government move from reactive firefighting to systemic change. The real work is not gathering detail (there is plenty). The real work is simplification: deciding what to touch first. Section 6 is built for exactly that.

2. The problem, defined

2.1 The one-line thesis

Air pollution and municipal waste in Delhi are not two problems but one interlocked crisis, bound by the same structural constraints: unfavourable winter geography, a regional airshed that ignores Delhi's borders, and a governance habit of emergency reaction over year-round systemic change. Fixing the air requires fixing the waste loop, and both require acting beyond Delhi's municipal limits.

2.2 Air, in numbers

  • Delhi has been the world's most polluted capital every year from 2018 to 2024 (IQAir). Annual PM2.5 is ~105 ug/m3, roughly 2.6x the national standard and ~20x the WHO guideline (CREA 2025).
  • There is real improvement off a catastrophic base: the government's Jan-to-Nov 2025 average PM2.5 (85) and AQI (187) were the best for that window since 2018 barring the 2020 lockdown, and the long arc is down from ~150 ug/m3 in 2013-15. (Caveat: the Jan-Nov window excludes December, Delhi's worst month; calendar-year figures are higher.)
  • The winter mechanism: from October to February the mixing layer collapses from ~2,000 m to under 200 m, wind stalls, and the city's effective air volume shrinks ~90%, multiplying the same emissions 5x to 10x. 25 to 30% of winter PM2.5 is secondary aerosol formed in the air itself.
  • Health: peer-reviewed work attributes ~11.5% of Delhi's deaths (~12,000/year) to PM2.5 (Lancet Planetary Health 2024); other estimates run higher. ~29% of Delhi adolescents show spirometry-defined airflow obstruction (Lung Care Foundation).

2.3 Waste, in numbers

  • Delhi generates ~11,850 tonnes per day (TPD) of municipal solid waste and processes only ~64% (~7,611 TPD), dumping a ~4,200 TPD gap every single day at landfills that are already full.
  • The three landfills (Ghazipur, Bhalswa, Okhla) held ~28 million tonnes at peak; ~11.5 million tonnes still remained as of Feb 2026 despite record biomining. Ghazipur grew from 65 m to 67 m even while being mined.
  • Separately, Delhi has India's largest sewage-treatment gap (~2,562 MLD), and contributes ~76% of the Yamuna's pollution load.

2.4 The interlock (how waste becomes air pollution)

  • Landfill methane and fires: mixed wet waste decays anaerobically, producing methane that fuels spontaneous, toxic, year-round underground fires. A single 2021 Ghazipur super-emitter event released 156 tonnes of methane per hour.
  • Open burning of uncollected/unsegregated waste (including plastic, e-waste cables, and dry leaves) is a direct source of particulate matter and dioxins.
  • Waste-to-energy plants burning mixed, wet, low-calorific waste run inefficiently and have documented dioxin/furan violations (Okhla measured 890% over limit).
  • Construction and demolition (C&D) debris is a leading source of the road and airborne dust that GRAP scrambles to suppress each winter.

3. What is in our control, and what is not

A task force earns its keep by working the controllables that also have a willing owner, and by managing (not fighting) the givens. This is the single most important slide for the government room.

3.1 The givens (not in Delhi's control, or not changeable on a project timescale)

Given Why it is a given Implication
Winter meteorology / boundary-layer collapse Physics of the season Cannot be engineered away; must reduce the emissions that get trapped
Regional airshed ~23 to 24% of Delhi's annual PM2.5 (up to ~70% in winter) comes from Haryana, Punjab, UP Delhi-only action caps out; airshed-scale action delivers 3 to 4x more benefit
Transboundary dust (summer) Dust blows in from outside the region Local dust control helps but will not zero out summer PM10
Multi-state jurisdiction Delhi is a UT surrounded by other states; 3 civic bodies within Needs coordination bodies (CAQM) and inter-state buy-in
Legacy stock ~11.5 MT already in the landfills Remediation is a multi-year drawdown, not a switch

3.2 The controllables (Delhi and partners can act)

Controllable Current state Leverage
Source segregation Citywide ~55% (MCD ~55 to 59%; self-reports higher) The master key: unlocks composting, recycling, cleaner WtE
Local transport / diesel black carbon Transport is the largest single local PM2.5 emitter (~28 to 39%); ~80% of BC from diesel Targeted, high-impact, has policy momentum (EV, freight)
Road and C&D dust ~6,000 to 6,500 TPD C&D; ~64 to 67% of NCAP money already goes here Diminishing returns on more dust spend; better process control needed
Open waste and landfill burning Chronic, under-measured Direct air win; ties waste fix to air fix
Sewage treatment ~2,562 MLD gap; only 237 of ~565 MGD treated meets standard Yamuna + methane + public-health co-benefit
Informal-sector integration 150,000+ waste pickers divert 15 to 20% of recyclables, largely excluded Cheap, proven capacity waiting to be formalised
Data trust CAG (2025): stations mis-sited, AQI unreliable Fixing measurement is a precondition for accountability

"Waste" is ten different problems. Each stream below has its own quantity, destination, failure point, owner, and link to air. This table is the working map for the task force. Reading the table: the master lever is stream 1 to 3 segregation (fix it and 2, 3, and half of 10 improve at once). The fastest air wins are stream 4, 7, and open burning. The biggest unowned gap is stream 5 and 9. The cheapest latent capacity is the informal sector across 2, 3, 4.

6. From problem to solution: the structure that does the simplifying

The task force should run every candidate intervention through one triage, so the room converges instead of expanding. Do-first = in our control x high impact x has a willing owner.

The 5-step exercise structure: 1. Define the problem (Sections 2 to 4 of this note): agreed baseline. 2. Separate givens from controllables (Section 3): decide the playing field. 3. Decompose the waste and pick target streams (Section 4): choose the battles. 4. Map owners and partners (Section 5): assign every chosen lever a name. 5. Match and sequence solutions to owners, filtered by feasibility x impact x buy-in.

An illustrative first-cut of the solution space (candidates, not commitments; each needs an owner and a buy-in check):

Ward-level decentralised composting + segregation drive Wet/organic (1), segregation Medium High (unlocks 2,3,10) High (visible, local)
Formalise + equip informal recyclers (Chintan model, scaled) Dry, plastic, e-waste (2,3,4) High Medium-High High (jobs + cheap capacity)
E-waste hotspot clean-tech + safe dismantling (Seelampur) E-waste (4) Medium High (air + health) Medium (livelihoods sensitive)

Governance proposal: RF + RM convene; a small task force of named partners owns 2 to 3 "do-first" levers; pilot-first, measure honestly, then scale the ones that earn buy-in.

7. Data gaps and honesty flags (say these out loud in the room)

  • MSW composition rests on a dated (2005) characterisation. A fresh Delhi waste-characterisation study is a cheap, high-value early task.
  • Plastic tonnage is genuinely contested (~690 vs ~1,145 TPD).
  • E-waste, sanitary, and domestic-hazardous volumes for Delhi are not reliably quantified in public data.
  • Biomedical waste has no compiled figures post-2017 (DPCC transparency gap).
  • Segregation and collection are largely self-reported near-100%, contradicted by the 64% processing rate: treat with caution.
  • Air data integrity itself is under a cloud (CAG 2025: mis-sited stations).
  • Landfill "cleared" totals from political statements are internally inconsistent with "remaining" figures.

These gaps are not a reason to wait; they are the first work items. Naming them builds credibility with the government room.
